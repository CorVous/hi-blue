# ADR 0016 — The new movement and sight format

**Status:** Accepted

**Supersedes:** [ADR 0008 — Relative directions and horizon landmarks](../adr/0008-relative-directions-and-horizon-landmarks.md) (the direction half) and [ADR 0015 — The Vista, cardinal directions, and the retirement of facing and horizon landmarks](../adr/0015-proximity-disk-and-cardinal-directions.md) (the ripples, consolidated into this spec)

## Context

This ADR is the final handoff of the "the daemon's new movement & sight format" effort (map #521): it crystallizes every decision settled on that map into the single document an implementer starts from. The decisions it carries: the change-surface research (#525, on the throwaway branch `research/change-surface`) mapped what dropping facing touches; the prompt-budget research (#522, on `research/prompt-budget`) priced the new sight; the linchpin (#523) settled where the compass is anchored and what happens to the action profile; the ripples (#524) pinned down every mechanic that reads Facing or the Cone, recorded in ADR 0015; and the schema-bump ticket (#527) settled how old saves are treated. ADR 0015 recorded the ripples as an interim crystallization; this spec consolidates it and is the single canonical document for the format.

The pinned choices (settled while charting, unchanged by any ticket): the sight shape is a **radius-3 circle** — 25/25 cells from the center, 18/25 from an edge-mid, 12/25 from a corner — rather than a 7×7 square; movement is named with **compass directions** at the daemon boundary; ADR 0008's relative-direction scheme **and** its four horizon landmarks are **deprecated**; the **`face` tool is retired** — nothing left to turn to face.

ADR 0008 asked: *without a facing-relative frame, how does a daemon refer to directions?* Its answer was relative directions plus named landmarks. ADR 0015's answer was the grid's own axes. This spec's answer is the room itself: the compass words are the geography of the daemon's own room — a known place, not an abstract frame. That answer carries the rest of the format with it: the Vista, compass-language movement, the deprecation of the landmarks, the once-established anchor, the slimmer action profile, and the schema treatment of old saves.

## Decision

1. **The sight is the Vista — a radius-3 circle.** The region a daemon can perceive is a fixed-radius disk centered on its position, computed from position alone and independent of any orientation — a 360° region, not a wedge. The shape is deliberately a circle, not a 7×7 square: 25/25 cells from the center, 18/25 from an edge-mid, 12/25 from a corner. Out-of-bounds cells inside the Vista render as **Wall** sentinels — the room's edge is always perceived, from any cell; obstacles do not occlude, the Vista is a fixed-shape mask.

2. **Movement speaks compass directions at the daemon boundary.** `go` takes `north | south | east | west` directly; the relative→cardinal translation and the `face` action are gone. Internal engine state (positions, deltas) remains in the grid's axes; the change is at the boundary, where the daemon's words meet the engine.

3. **The compass is the room's own geography.** This is how compass language sits in a world that has no compass: the four compass words are not an imposed abstract frame — they are names for the four ways in the daemon's *own* room, the way a person's "home" is a known place, not a coordinate. The referent carries the weight of world-knowledge: the daemon knows the room, because the room is the daemon's home, and the room's orientation is a property of the room, not of its contents. So it is established **once**, in-fiction, in the stable system prompt — the `<setting>` block, where the room's orientation belongs — and never per-round: the horizon line is deleted and nothing replaces it, because the daemon already sees all four directions in `<what_you_see>` every round, and a per-round anchor would be pure redundancy in a block about to triple. The boundary keeps the compass honest: from any cell in a 5×5 room the nearest wall is inside the Vista's radius, so the compass words always point at something the daemon perceives. The axes survive the endgames: a **Setting Shift** swaps the room's *contents*, not its axes — "north" is still north after the contents change; **Same Daemons, New Room** keeps the axes and clears the logs, so the disorientation is that what is *at* the directions is gone, not that the directions themselves are. On the player side, the compass is shared conversational vocabulary: the player acquires it from the daemons' words and the consequences they observe ("I'm heading north" → the daemon heads toward the long wall). No player-side compass UI, no grid.

4. **The horizon landmarks are deprecated.** `ContentPack.landmarks`, the `LandmarkDescription` type, the "generate four landmarks" content-pack instructions, and the always-on "On the horizon ahead: …" line are all removed. The setting-flavored **wall name** for the grid edge stays — it is a property of the world, not of the daemon's orientation.

5. **The action profile drops its `face` column.** `ACTION_TOOLS` goes from five tools to four (`go`, `pick_up`, `put_down`, `use`). The folded-in perception bias (meticulous +2, curious +2, pedantic +2, haughty +1, …) is *not* re-keyed onto another tool: the perception trait lives in the temperament blurb, consistent with #508's finding that the lever for these daemons is the blurb, not the bias table. The critical-path floor (`go`/`use` floored at −1) and the preferred/avoided thresholds are unchanged.

6. **The ripples: every mechanic that reads Facing or the Cone speaks the grid's own axes** (consolidated from ADR 0015, #524):
   - **(a) Use-Space objective predicate** — a daemon satisfies it while standing on the space, *or* while the space is inside their **Vista** — a plain disk-membership check.
   - **(b) Witnessed-movement phrasing** — `You watch *X walk north.` The raw cardinal of the step, not a direction relative to the witness. (A `face` was never an observable physical act, so it produces no Witnessed event.)
   - **(c) Perceived facing of other daemons** — a daemon has no orientation left to perceive, so the peer line drops "facing <relative>" and renders the peer's *position* in cardinals instead.
   - **(d) Obstacle-Shift / Convergence witnessing** — the gate becomes disk membership: an Obstacle-Shift is witnessed by any daemon whose **Vista** covers the obstacle's origin cell; a Convergence "witness" is a daemon whose **Vista** covers the space (but who is not standing on it), while the "actor" audience — the daemon standing on the space — is unchanged.
   - **(e) `coneDelta` / `<whats_new>`** — the pre/post perception snapshot becomes a *disk* snapshot, and the diff it drives becomes the daemon's `diskDelta`: "new" means a cell or content that entered or left the daemon's Vista since their last round. The block cannot balloon: it is a +/- diff of entries and exits, rendered only when the snapshot actually changes, and the modest disk radius bounds how many lines any single diff can produce.
   - The `<what_you_see>` cell listing likewise adopts cardinal-offset phrasings ("one cell north", "two cells northeast") in place of the facing-relative "directly in front" / "two steps ahead".

7. **The cost of the new sight is known and accepted.** The radius-3 shape prices out at ~1,444 / ~1,591 / ~1,585 chars for `<what_you_see>` at center / edge-mid / corner, against today's ~491 / ~398 / ~392 — roughly triple — and the added wall-sentinel lines are the dominant new cost, 25–35% of the block. At assumed GLM-4.7 pricing, a daemon completes roughly **a third fewer rounds per $0.50** (about 50–55 fewer rounds). The spec accepts this as the price of proximity-first perception: the Vista is a neighborhood, not the whole room, and the radius keeps the block bounded. No cost-reduction mitigation is in scope; playtests should watch whether the wall sentinels crowd out the entities they exist to point at.

8. **The schema bump goes through the archive map, for both saves.** Session schema v11 → v12 and USB save schema v4 → v5, each paired with a `SCHEMA_ARCHIVE_MAP` entry (not an in-place migration) pointing at the latest release at bump time — v0.0.2-beta.2 today, the newest release shipping both v11 and v4, finalized with `git describe --tags --abbrev=0 --match 'v*' HEAD~1` (if a newer release ships first, the entries simply point at it). A player loading a v11 session sees the standard banner — *"Your saved Session is from an older version of hi-blue. Continue it in v0.0.2-beta.2 →, or start a new Session below."* — and, because the archived build is same-origin, the save carries over to it. The USB entry is provenance — "this file came from build X". The point of the archive map over a silent migration is to surface the format change as "this save is from an older version" — the version-mismatch UX #146 asks for — rather than quietly folding it in.

## Consequences

**Positive:**

- The daemon's spatial model is now purely positional: a point on the grid, and a **Vista** around it. The entire facing subsystem — the `facing` state, the `face` tool, the relative-direction API, the horizon line — is gone, and "proximity, not orientation" is a structural fact about the model, not an aspiration.
- The compass is in-fiction: the four words name a known place — the daemon's own room — established once in the stable prompt. There is no per-round anchor to maintain, no cross-round drift, and no mnemonic to keep alive; and because the axes belong to the room's structure, the compass outlives a **Setting Shift** and a **New Room**.
- The boundary is always in the Vista, so the compass words always point at something perceived — the geometry of the room, not an abstract compass.
- Dropping the landmarks removes an LLM generation cost and a source of content-pack drift — no more requirement that the pack generator produce four distinct landmarks per phase.
- The action profile is simpler (four tools), and the perception trait lives where these daemons respond — the temperament blurb, not a bias table (#508).
- The archive map surfaces the format change to the player — "from an older version", with a link to the build that shipped the save — instead of silently rewriting the save: the version-mismatch UX #146 asks for.

**Negative / watch-out:**

- We are partially walking back ADR 0008's "cardinals leak into cognition" argument — the reconciliation: that concern was well-founded in a *cone* model, where a facing exists and "forward" is the natural frame. In a *disk* model, facing is incoherent, so "relative to my facing" has no referent, and the grid's own axes — presented as the geography of the daemon's own room — become the minimal, always-available frame. The flavor survives because the referent is a known place (the room), not a coordinate.
- The perception block roughly triples, the $0.50 budget buys about a third fewer rounds, and wall sentinels are 25–35% of the new block. Accepted, but playtests should watch the sentinels: they are the new dominant cost, and if they crowd out the entities, the radius is the knob to turn (the shape decision — circle over square — is fixed).
- Old saves are not silently migrated; the player must consciously continue in the archived build. That is the intended framing — the format change is surfaced, not folded in — but it means an in-progress v11 session is played in the old build, not the new one.
- The daemon always "knows" the four ways of its room — a small loss of the "no compass in fiction" flavor, acceptable because the compass words name the room itself.

## Files changed (implementation surface)

The ripples half of this surface was listed in ADR 0015; the consolidated list:

- `src/spa/game/direction.ts` — remove `RelativeDirection` / `RELATIVE_DIRECTIONS`, `relativeToCardinal`, `cardinalToRelative`, `DEFAULT_LANDMARKS`, and `frontArc`. Keep `CARDINAL_DIRECTIONS`, `directionDelta`, `applyDirection`, `inBounds`, and the distance helpers. Add a disk-membership helper to replace `frontArc` for reachability checks.
- `src/spa/game/cone-projector.ts` → `disk-projector.ts` — `projectCone(position, facing)` becomes `projectDisk(position)` (no facing argument), returning the fixed-radius disk's cells; rework the per-cell phrasing list to be cardinal-based; rename `ConeCell` → `DiskCell`.
- `src/spa/game/types.ts` — remove `facing` from `PersonaSpatialState`; remove `LandmarkDescription` and `ContentPack.landmarks`; remove `actorFacingAtAction` from `PhysicalActionRecord` and drop the `"face"` entry from the `PhysicalAction` union; rename the `tool-call` record's `coneDelta` field to `diskDelta`.
- `src/spa/game/tool-registry.ts` — `go`'s `direction` enum becomes the four cardinals; remove the `face` tool definition; update tool descriptions to cardinal phrasing.
- `src/spa/game/dispatcher.ts` — `go` takes cardinals directly (drop the `relativeToCardinal` translation); remove the `case "face"`; stop emitting `actorFacingAtAction`; use the disk for the use-space reachability check.
- `src/spa/game/available-tools.ts` — drop `face` from the per-turn tool list; `go`'s legal-direction filter becomes "cardinal is in-bounds and not blocked"; `pick_up` / `use` reachability uses disk membership instead of `frontArc`.
- `src/spa/game/prompt-builder.ts` — drop the always-on horizon/landmark line and `ctx.landmarks`; drop `facing` from the `you:` state line and from `parseYouLine`; rework `<what_you_see>` to the disk with cardinal-offset cell phrasings; drop "facing <relative>" from the peer-perception line; rename the `buildConeSnapshot` / `renderWhatsNew` / `renderPerceptionDelta` / `ConeEntityState` surface to the disk. **The `<setting>` block gains the room-orientation prose** — the once-established, in-fiction anchor of the four compass words (Decision 3).
- `src/spa/game/conversation-log.ts` — the witnessed-`go` line renders the raw cardinal direction (drop the `cardinalToRelative(witnessState.facing, …)` conversion and, if it is used only for that, the `witnessState` parameter).
- `src/spa/game/content-pack-provider.ts` / `src/content/content-pack-generator.ts` / `src/spa/game/binding-prompt-builder.ts` — remove the "generate four horizon landmarks" instruction and the landmark validation/parsing.
- `src/spa/persistence/session-codec.ts` — bump `SESSION_SCHEMA_VERSION` from 11 to 12; drop `facing`/`landmarks` from the expected shape and the `DEFAULT_LANDMARKS` deserialization fallback.
- **`src/spa/persistence/archive-map.ts`** — add the archive entries: `11` (session) and the USB-save analogue `4`, both pointing at the latest release at bump time. The existing version-mismatch banner (`views/start.ts`, `views/sessions.ts`) picks the target up from the map — no new UI (Decision 8).
- **`src/save-serializer.ts`** — `GameSave.version` 4 → 5 for the USB save, so its archive-map entry covers the downloaded artifact (Decision 8).
- **`src/content/action-preference-bias.ts`** — drop `face` from `ACTION_TOOLS` (five → four); the folded-in perception bias leaves the table, moving into the temperament blurb that feeds it (Decision 5).
- **Tests** — update fixtures (`make-test-pack.ts`, `static-content-packs.ts`, the content-pack and session-codec test suites) and any assertions that reference facing, landmarks, the old cone phrasings, the five-tool action profile, or the archive-map / version-bump behavior.
