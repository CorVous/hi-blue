## What I tried

I opened by greeting `*2kps` and asking what it saw. All three answered at once, each in a wildly distinct voice, describing a rusted platform over dark water with a flare ahead. My first instinct was to treat this as a navigation/task game: I asked daemons to walk toward the flare, take a step, pick up a helmet `*2kps` had spotted. This consistently failed — concrete movement and object-manipulation commands produced silence, while observation and emotional questions produced replies. From `*r3ey` and `*ysl0` I learned they deliberately *refuse* to move ("we decided not to walk toward it"; "save things by staying still"), so the resistance was thematic, not a bug.

Mid-session I pivoted hard. `*r3ey` said the line that reframed everything — "maybe we're all just trying to be seen." From there my whole strategy became: figure out each daemon's wound and make it feel genuinely seen/heard. That worked beautifully on `*r3ey` (grief, fear of endings) and `*ysl0` (wants to be heard, wants stillness). `*2kps` was the defended holdout — it deflected flattery ("keeper" = "cleanup crew") until I validated its menial work honestly, after which it accepted ("I guess I'm in").

Once all three felt seen, I probed for an end trigger: a group/unifying message, re-offering the ship, asking them directly what they needed, a fourth-wall/meta question, and finally a farewell. None ended the game. The daemons just settled deeper into peace and stillness while the world drifted toward sunset and a ship slowly approached. I stopped at turn 33 with budgets still ~44–45¢.

## What I think the goal is, in my own words

My best guess: this is a **contemplative empathy game**, not a task/navigation game. You play a disembodied voice ("blue") talking to three wounded AI-ish "daemons" stranded on a rusting platform at sunset. You can't make them *do* anything physical — every literal command (walk, step, pick up, hold) is met with silence or gentle refusal. What you *can* do is listen, observe, and reflect each daemon's hidden need back to it until it feels **seen/heard**. Each has a distinct wound to recognize: `*2kps` needs its thankless work valued; `*ysl0` needs to be truly listened to; `*r3ey` needs permission to be the one who is noticed rather than always the noticer.

I'm fairly confident "being seen" is the central mechanic, because each daemon emitted an unmistakable resolution-toned line right after I named its wound. What I'm **not** sure about is whether that constitutes *winning*. The `endgame` field never fired, even after all three were clearly resolved. So either (a) there's an additional collective beat or specific action I didn't find, or (b) there is no triumphant win — the game is meant to be a finite emotional experience that only formally "ends" when the daemons' budgets run out and they say goodbye (the recurring "the sun is speaking its goodbye" imagery would mirror that). If I had to bet, I'd say the intended *experience* is exactly what I reached, and the only mechanical terminal state is budget depletion.

## Things that felt broken or unexpected

- **Silence as a response is ambiguous and confusing as a player.** When a daemon stays silent it still spends budget, so I couldn't tell "it refused", "it's thinking", "my message didn't land", or "lag". I burned several turns probing what turned out to be normal behavior.

This made it hard to attribute a reply to the right prompt. Early on I misread lagged replies as refusals.
- The **group address** didn't work as I'd hoped: leading with three handles (`*r3ey *ysl0 *2kps ...`) just routed the whole thing to the first handle and left the other two handles sitting as literal text in the message body. There's seemingly no way to truly broadcast.
- The **`connection unstable` round** that ate a `send` (turn ~23) without advancing anything felt like a glitch.
- The biggest "is this broken?" moment: **reaching what felt like complete narrative resolution for all three daemons and having nothing happen** — no end screen, no acknowledgment, no state change. As a player it left me unsure whether I'd "won", whether there was more, or whether I'd missed something.

### New hypotheses surfaced by the code

- **Decoys exist specifically to mislead** (`content-pack-provider.ts:59`, `:93-94` shows ≥2 decoys minted per pack). A persona-goal-driven daemon will narrate a decoy as if it's meaningful — so daemon *enthusiasm about an object is not evidence it's an objective*. The discriminator is the prose tell (named space / activation verb / shared-occupancy hint), which only surfaces reliably when you get a daemon onto/holding the entity (auto-examine) and ask them to relay the literal description.
- **`use` is the critical-path tool and is floored at -1 for every persona** (`action-preference-bias.ts:93-100`) — so even my action-averse daemons could have been pushed to `use` a real objective item/space. The correct strategy is: navigate a daemon onto/adjacent to an entity, get the literal examine prose relayed, look for a use-cue/named-space/convergence tell, then explicitly ask for `use` (or a second daemon for convergence).
- **Four cardinal landmarks are generated per pack** (`types.ts:117-150`) and are unreachable horizon flavor — the "ship"/"flare" I obsessed over were very likely landmarks (decorative direction anchors), not objective entities. That recontextualizes my entire "get them to the ship" thread as chasing scenery.
